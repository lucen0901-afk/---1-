Why Drains Block in Guangzhou Apartments

Updated 13 September 2026 11 minute read By the Guangzhou Home Help team

A slow drain is one of those problems that is easy to ignore for weeks and then impossible to ignore for a single morning. In Guangzhou the causes are fairly consistent, and knowing which one you have tells you whether it is a five-minute job, a call-out, or something the building needs to deal with.

The Six Reasons Drains Block Here

1. Cooking oil that sets as it cools

Cooking oil leaves the pan warm and liquid. By the time it has travelled a few metres along a narrow kitchen waste pipe, it is cooling and sticking to the pipe wall. It does not block anything the first time. It builds a layer that slowly narrows the pipe until the day a rice grain or a tea leaf finishes the job. This is the single most common cause of kitchen drain blockages in Guangzhou apartments, and the hardest to prevent without changing a habit.

2. Hair and soap in shower traps

Mechanical and predictable. Long hair combined with soap residue forms a mat that catches everything else. The trap is designed to catch it — the problem is that most people never open the trap until the drain has already stopped.

3. Scale and sediment

Guangzhou's water is hard. Over years, mineral scale reduces the internal diameter of waste pipes, and sediment settles in horizontal runs. This is gradual and mostly affects older buildings, but it explains why a drain that has become progressively slower over two years is unlikely to be fixed permanently by a quick poke.

4. Long, shallow horizontal runs

Waste pipes need fall, and sometimes they do not have enough of it. In apartments where the drain has to travel a long way to the stack with only a centimetre or two of drop per metre, water never fully clears the pipe between uses and debris settles out permanently. This is a design problem, not a maintenance one, and it is why the same drain in the same flat blocks every few months no matter how careful the tenants are.

5. Shared stacks in older blocks

In many buildings from the 1980s and 1990s — a lot of Yuexiu, parts of Liwan and the inner streets of Haizhu — several floors share one waste stack. When it blocks, it blocks for everyone at once, and the water shows up first in the lowest flat. If two or more of your fixtures are draining badly at the same time, this is almost certainly what you are looking at, and no amount of work inside your own apartment will fix it.

6. Monsoon backflow and construction debris

When the city's storm drains fill during a heavy rain event, water can push back up through ground-floor floor drains. Separately, if your building or your own apartment has been renovated recently, plaster, tile adhesive and grout are frequent contributors to blockages that appear a few months after the work finished.

What You Can Safely Try Yourself

Four steps, in order. Stop as soon as one of them works — and stop the moment you suspect the problem is bigger than your own fixture.

  1. Boiling water with a squirt of dish soap. Pour it in slowly, in two or three stages, waiting a minute between each. It dissolves grease and shifts light surface buildup. Effective on kitchen drains, useless on hair.
  2. A plunger, used properly. A plunger only works if it seals. Remove the drain cover, wet the rim of the plunger, and make sure any overflow opening is blocked with a wet cloth — otherwise you are just pumping air from one place to another. Short, firm strokes, then lift quickly.
  3. Open the trap. Under most basins there is a bottle trap or a P-trap that unscrews by hand. Put a bucket underneath first, undo it slowly, and clean out what you find. This is where most basin blockages actually are, and it takes ten minutes.
  4. A plumber's snake — the flexible hand-tool, not the machine. Useful for a short blockage a metre or two in, and only on a drain you can physically reach.

What never to do: caustic drain cleaner in an old building

The powder and the liquid you can buy in convenience stores work by generating heat and dissolving organic matter. They do not know the difference between the blockage and the pipe. In a building with cast-iron or older PVC waste pipes, repeated use creates leaks where there was previously only a blockage — and if it does not clear the drain, it leaves a pipe full of caustic liquid for whoever opens it next.

If you have already used one and the drain is still blocked, say so when you call. It changes how the job is approached.

When to Stop and Call a Professional

Five signals that mean the DIY phase is over:

  • The same drain has blocked three or more times in a year. Something structural is wrong — fall, scale, or root ingress. Clearing it again is a subscription, not a repair.
  • Two or more fixtures are affected. A basin and a shower both draining slowly points to the main line or the stack. This is the classic misdiagnosis: people clear the basin trap, conclude it is fixed, and the water reappears next week through the floor drain.
  • Water rises in one fixture when you use another. Washing machine water appearing in the shower, for example. That is a downstream blockage, by definition beyond your fixture.
  • You can smell the sewer. A persistent smell means the trap is being emptied by pressure rather than holding its water seal.
  • It has been slow ever since a renovation. Debris from the work is very likely still in the pipe, and it will not dissolve.

What a Professional Actually Does

There are three tools and they do genuinely different things, which is why "the plumber cleared it and it blocked again" usually means the wrong one was used.

  • An auger or drain snake cuts a channel through the blockage. Fast, effective at restoring flow immediately, and completely ineffective at cleaning the pipe wall. This is the cheap visit that comes back.
  • High-pressure jetting scours the full circumference of the pipe, removing grease layers and scale as well as the obstruction. Slower, needs an access point, and is the reason a properly jetted drain usually stays clear.
  • A camera inspection tells you why it blocked — a collapsed section, root ingress, a low-slope run, or a build-up of construction debris. Worth doing on any drain that has blocked more than once, because it converts guesswork into a decision.

Preventing the Next One

  • Kitchen: pour fat into a jar and bin it, never down the sink. Use a mesh strainer, and every couple of weeks run a full sink of very hot water through the drain to move the grease layer along.
  • Bathroom: fit a hair catcher in the shower drain and empty it weekly. It costs almost nothing and it is the single most effective drain maintenance you can do.
  • Balcony and outdoor drains: sweep leaves and dust away before the rainy season rather than during it. In a villa or a ground-floor flat, check the gully outside as well as the one inside.
  • After a renovation: have the drains in the affected rooms checked and jetted before you settle the final payment. Debris left in a pipe is far cheaper to remove at that point than three months later.
  • If the drain is already slow: deal with it while it is slow. A drain that is 70% blocked is a small job; the same drain on a Sunday evening with guests arriving is not.

The one-minute version

Kitchen sink slow? Almost always grease — hot water and soap first. Shower slow? Almost always hair — open the trap. Several fixtures slow at once? Stop, and call someone, because it is the main line and nothing you do inside the flat will fix it.
